Mine, build, upgrade your broken android body, and explore infinite vertical worlds of stone, lava, and ruins.

## Long Description


### About This Game

You awaken in ruins, a broken android with one working arm, a fractured leg, and the faint glow of a fusion core running low. Around you, the world stretches infinitely in all directions — carved from noise, stone, and molten rivers. Nothing here is random. Every ore, every drop of water, every crumbling factory is reproducible down to the tick.

LithicRivers is an ASCII sandbox roguelike where your body is the crafting system, your limbs are your inventory, and survival means rebuilding yourself piece by piece.

### Features

Deterministic Sandbox
Every “random” element — from mining results to dungeon layouts — is seeded and fully reproducible. Share a world seed and get the exact same experience, tick by tick. Perfect for speedrunners, challenge seeds, and theorycrafters.

Body as Progression
Start damaged. Find parts. Repair your limbs, graft augments, and rebuild your android body into something powerful, strange, or barely held together. Your body is your inventory and your main crafting system.

Vertical Exploration
The world is infinite and three-dimensional. Carve up through forests and ruins, down into frozen wastelands, abandoned mines, and the molten “Lithic Rivers” biome — underground caverns of lava, rare ore, and hostile machines.

Automation & Emergence
Conveyors, fluids, and procedural systems interact in unexpected ways. Build, automate, or exploit the physics of ASCII itself.

ASCII Worldbuilding
From tiny 1x1 blocks to detailed 12x8 sprites, the entire game is rendered in expressive ASCII art. Retro roguelike feel, modern depth.

Why LithicRivers?

If Minecraft is about freedom, Factorio about optimization, Caves of Qud about narrative strangeness, and Dwarf Fortress about simulation, then LithicRivers is about determinism and embodiment. It’s a survival sandbox where nothing is left to chance — and where rebuilding your body is as important as rebuilding the world.

Target price: accessible indie ($3). Optimized to run in your terminal or on a potato laptop, but deep enough to live alongside the great roguelikes.

Platforms: Built natively in Rust for Windows, Debian Linux, and MacOS.
